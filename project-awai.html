Quick Response & Information App

Two separate Android apps, built for one client as part of the AWAI project. Quick Response helps tamariki (children) and rangatahi (young people) reach a trusted adult quickly; the Information App is for their parents and caregivers. Both work in English and te reo Māori.

01 Two apps

Two apps for two audiences.

Two Android apps serve different audiences within the AWAI client project.

Quick Response is for young people. It works out the user’s age group from their date of birth: tamariki aged 5–12 get a simpler, illustrated interface, and rangatahi aged 13–17 get a more grown-up one. Both versions lead to the same idea: a trusted contact is always a short, deliberate hold away, and a short Help Me Decide quiz helps a young person work out what kind of help they need.

The Information App (launcher name Pārongo) is for parents and caregivers. It explains uplifting, the process and legal rights in plain language, with a short quiz. It is shown here as the AWAI team’s app.

02 What I built

In Quick Response: the data layer and the whole children’s interface.

  • The Room data layer: the database, its entities and the migrations.
  • The first-launch setup: six screens, with helpers and their English and te reo Māori strings.
  • The contacts module, Edit My Details and the Profile screens.
  • The Help Me Decide quiz screens (the decision logic is the team lead’s).
  • The entire Tamariki interface, the age routers and the settings.
  • The first Home screen and the hold-to-alert prototype.

03 The team’s part

What others built.

The team lead built the app shell, navigation, messaging and permissions, caregiver logic, and final Help Me Decide logic. My contribution focused on data, setup, contacts, profiles, quiz screens and the Tamariki interface.

05 Information App

Information for parents and caregivers.

The Information App explains, in English and te reo Māori, what uplifting is, the five types of uplifting and the process step by step, then the legal rights and responsibilities of parents and of caregivers on two tabs. A quiz draws 10 of its 30 yes/no questions at random and explains each answer. A first-run profile (name, date of birth, address, language) stays on the device. The wording is prototype content written for the client project, not legal advice.

06 How it works

Small, offline and bilingual.

Quick Response:

  • Two activities (the splash and the host) with 24 fragment destinations in one navigation graph.
  • Room database version 5 with three tables: the user profile, key contacts and app settings.
  • The age group is calculated from the date of birth on every launch, so it is never stored or out of date.
  • All 476 translatable strings exist in te reo Māori; the language choice is stored and applied app-wide.

Information App:

  • One host activity with 13 fragments in a navigation graph, and a splash screen.
  • A Room database (version 1) with a single profile row; profile and quiz screens follow MVVM with ViewModels and StateFlow.
  • The quiz reads its 30 questions from a bundled JSON file and shuffles 10 per attempt.
  • 203 of its 204 translatable strings exist in te reo Māori, switched live from the toolbar.

07 Outcome

Two age groups, a familiar path to help.

Quick Response brings age-based setup, trusted contacts and bilingual screens into one flow. Building the Tamariki interface meant adapting the wording, controls and layout for younger users while keeping the same core tasks.
